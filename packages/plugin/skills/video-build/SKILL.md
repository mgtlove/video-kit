# Build a video

Runs in the director's chat (the one with the kit's repo). The producer and the subject expert have their own skills (`video-brief`, `video-fact-check`); this one is the order end to end.

A video is a folder made by `vkit new <name>` (or `vkit new <name> --app family/tool` when a recreated app exists): its script, words, screen, timeline and the record of its choices. The rig page is the footage, not a preview; anything wrong in it is wrong in the delivered video. The product screen is never recorded; it is recreated in HTML from still screenshots with an id each.

## Order

1. **The idea, in the subject expert's chat.** `video-brief` writes `brief.md` (who it is for, what they can do after, the points) and the screens the teaching needs (`capture-request.md`, in the app folder when the app does not exist yet). Nothing starts in this chat before that file exists; `vkit handoffs` says so.
2. **Evidence.** The capture browser first (`capture-walkthrough`: `vkit shoot` on the machine with the browser, the person signed in; the path plus one layer out, `docs/CAPTURE.md`); a walkthrough document when the person who has the tool is not at that machine (`video-capture`: `vkit capture <walkthrough.docx> --into <folder>`). Every picture is looked at before anything is recreated. A screen with no capture becomes a concept scene and a pickup request. The screen becomes an app folder (`video-recreate`) with its behaviour as data (`app-model`: `model.json`, which control on which state leads where), its own versioned asset, and `vkit app use family/tool` brings it into the video.
3. **The menu.** The `video-menu` skill: job and sources from `brief.md` (the audience and length are the expert's words), the rest by the defaults until a reason says otherwise; `vkit menu --show` must answer job and sources before the storyboard is asked for.
4. **Brief, storyboard, script.** `vkit brief` writes `brief-request.md` with the app's states in it; the subject expert's chat answers with the `video-brief` skill by writing `brief.md`, the storyboard rows and `capture-request.md` into the same folder; the `video-script` skill reads them, checks `storyboard-covered`, and writes the narration; the expert's `video-fact-check` writes `fact-check.md` beside it. The folder is the handoff and `vkit handoffs` says whose turn it is; nothing is pasted between chats. Pickups are captured and recreated before a sentence depends on them. The narration: `narration/part-N.md`, one sentence per line, one line per storyboard row, about 1000 characters a part at most so a part is cheap to make again, no sentence over 30 words, the whole video under about five minutes. `vkit narration` checks the limits, writes `narration/FULL.md`, writes the final sentences into the storyboard's Sentence column itself and logs every row whose words moved from the expert's in `narration/changes.md` (the expert's words as first seen, the director's latest); never edit the Sentence column by hand, so that log stays true. Get the words approved before any voice exists.
5. **Voice, measure, time.** The producer records each part (Voice Memos is fine; `voice/part-N.m4a`; `.wav`, `.mp3`, `.aac`, `.flac` and `.ogg` are read too), or a paid voice service with the person's yes in the conversation and `--approved` on the command; then the `video-measure` skill: `vkit measure` for the part lengths, `vkit time` for the second each sentence starts (word times from the clips, into the Start column). The clips are the clock; a re-recorded part re-times only itself. Beats are placed at the spoken word, never at an estimate.
6. **Screen and words.** `COPY` in `rig/index.html` holds every on-screen string. Recreate the screen from the captures only; keep the element ids the timeline points at.
7. **Timeline.** One `at()` per sentence at the row's Start, offset from its part's start, the sentence in the comment, mirrored in `storyboard.md` (`vkit render` makes the captions from it). A console video is a screen recording in stills: every change on screen has its cause on screen first, and the row's Action column says what that cause is. The behaviour is the app's (`model.json`, `docs/MODEL.md`), never written into the timeline: for a click or typing, `pointer(id)` glides to the control and `act(t, id)` lands near the end of the sentence (the control presses; the app's pressed look comes from a capture, the engine's darkening until then), and the model's answer follows on its own: a new page with its load and the chrome staying, a change in place, or the state's own text typed character by character into the field; `act(t, id, { when: 'taken' })` takes a conditional transition. For a scroll: `scrollPage(t, scrolledState, 3.2)`, which moves the page and its scrollbar in place; never a camera `travel` standing in for a scroll. Never a bare `state()` for a page change and never a field filled in a cut; `actions-in-model` fails a state change the model does not have. The console sits at 1:1 (`home()`); the camera pushes in only to read something small, 1.15 to 1.4, and comes home. Strokes (`ink`) mark the control a sentence is about. Over a recreated screen the row's Card words become a `note` (one line at 28 px, a leader to the element; the look draws it), never a `card`, which is for concept scenes. Name the leaf the sentence is about (a field, a radio, a button), never a whole section. A note named in the same beat as a camera move or a scroll waits for it to land. The engine always places the note somewhere (the nearest clear spot, else the side that covers least) and `cards-clear` fails when that spot covers text or a picture; when it fails at every scale the row allows, take the note out, let the sentence carry it, and mark the control with a stroke (`ink`) so the hold stays inside the tone's limit. Say in the delivery which rows got no note and why. `vkit check --quick` must show `actions-move` passing: every Action row changes the frame inside its beat. `docs/ENGINE.md` is the list.
8. **Look.** The `video-frames` skill until every frame is right; then the `video-check` skill: `vkit check --quick` while working, `vkit check` to land.
9. **Render.** `PW_CHANNEL=chrome vkit render` makes the MP4 with the voice at its part offsets and the VTT and SRT beside it; play it. `vkit publish` waits for a host adapter (roadmap milestone F) and is not built; the MP4 is handed over by the person.

## Rules that are not negotiable

- Never invent a screen detail. No capture, no chrome.
- Never fabricate what a product prints. Use a captured output word for word or cut to a concept scene.
- Colours, faces and brand are tokens: `rig/theme.css`, `rig/look.css` (`vkit look`), `rig/brand.css` (`vkit brand`); never a literal in the rig; never restyle anything under `#mock`.
- Never stretch or scale a part to fit; make the clip again and measure again.
- Never edit `rig/engine/` inside a video; change the kit, prove it with `npm test`, and run `vkit upgrade` in the video (the engine copied in, `VERSION` and `video.json.engine` written, the old copy kept in the video's `_suggested-trash/`).
- No real person's name on screen; an invented cast, reused across videos.
- Plain language, no em dashes, no mention of how the work was made in anything a viewer reads.
