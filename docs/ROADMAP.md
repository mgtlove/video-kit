# Roadmap

Each step lands with its proof: rendered frames compared, or a check that passes. Docs change in the same commit as the code.

1. Skeleton and docs. Done.
2. Core: clock and timeline (`at()` beats, parts), reset and seek, camera (`focusAt`, `focusEl`, `travel`, `home`, clamped to the frame, never under scale 1), scene and card helpers (the card places itself beside the element it names). Proof: `vkit new` creates a video from the starter and `vkit frames` writes 16 stills, one per beat plus part starts. Done.
3. Seek-correct motion: on a seek, beats older than the motion window are snapped, beats still in flight are re-fired with motion on and every animation they started is held at `t minus beat time`. Proof: `npm test` plays the starter for real to four moments (a scene crossfade, a card entrance, a camera move, a pull-back), reads the exact time each picture shows, seeks to it and compares pixels. Measured with engine 0.1.1: 0 of 2,073,600 pixels differ at all four moments. (The first measurement, with 0.1.0, was 0 at the card entrance, up to 6 levels on the crossfade and 225 edge pixels on the camera move; the residue was the compositor drawing held transitions on its own clock, closed in step 5.) Tolerance in the test: 0.05 percent of pixels over 8 levels. Done.
4. Seeded strokes (circle, frame, underline, arrow) that draw on with the voice; characters; the walkthrough layer (pointer, click, type). Proof: re-renders identical.
5. `vkit render`: every frame at 30 fps from the seek, streamed into ffmpeg (H.264, crf 18, 4:2:0, AAC); each `voice/part-N` clip placed at its part offset, a silent track when there are none; `out/<name>.vtt` and `.srt` from the storyboard's sentences and start times; `out/render.json` with the numbers. The MP4 is exactly the sum of the parts long: the clips are the clock, and a beat after the last clip (the starter's closing fade) is not footage. This is the piece that never shipped before. Proof: `npm test` renders the starter with three beep clips and checks that the MP4 is 2040 frames and 68.000 s; that the frame handed to ffmpeg at each of the four seek-test moments equals `vkit frames` there (0 pixels); that the frame decoded back out of the MP4 is the same picture under the codec (45.4 to 48.6 dB PSNR on the Mac's Chrome; a frame off by one measures 26 to 28 dB; the floor is 40); and that the beeps land at 0.300, 24.300 and 50.300 s. Two things found and fixed on the way, both in the kit, never in a video: a screenshot straight after a seek could show the frame before it (the main thread had committed, the compositor had not drawn; about 47,000 pixels off on the crossfade, worse under load), so the render adapter now opens the page 2 px taller, sets a 1 px marker strip on row 0 in the same task as the seek, waits until a screenshot of row 0 shows it, then takes rows 1 to 1080; and a held transition was drawn on the compositor's clock, stepping about every 10 ms with a phase that changed per browser session (up to 17 levels between runs), so engine 0.1.1 bakes every held transition's value inline on the main thread (`docs/ENGINE.md`). Rendering the 68 s starter took 165 s on the Mac (81 ms a frame, one core); each render writes its own time and machine to `out/render.json`. Done.
6. `vkit check`: offline, deterministic, seekable, seek-correct; the craft rules from `rules.json`; brand contrast.
7. Brand kit, looks, the menu, `vkit sync-reference`. The menu lists what the data files hold.
8. The plugin: skills over `vkit`, guardrails. Drafted 1 Oct 2026 in `packages/plugin/` (six skills, the guard hook with 21 passing cases); installs and is tried on a real video once the commands it calls exist.
9. `vkit capture`: the agent walks a task in a browser and writes tagged screenshots and the manifest.
10. The sample video (something I can capture freely), the README for a reader who has two minutes.
11. The MCP server; cloud render and a voice provider as adapters when they earn their place.
