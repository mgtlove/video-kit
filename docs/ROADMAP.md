# Roadmap

Each step lands with its proof: rendered frames compared, or a check that passes. Docs change in the same commit as the code.

1. Skeleton and docs. Done.
2. Core: clock and timeline (`at()` beats, parts), reset and seek, camera (`focusAt`, `focusEl`, `travel`, `home`, clamped to the frame, never under scale 1), scene and card helpers (the card places itself beside the element it names). Proof: `vkit new` creates a video from the starter and `vkit frames` writes 16 stills, one per beat plus part starts. Done.
3. Seek-correct motion: on a seek, beats older than the motion window are snapped, beats still in flight are re-fired with motion on and every animation they started is held at `t minus beat time`. Proof: `npm test` plays the starter for real to four moments (a scene crossfade, a card entrance, a camera move, a pull-back), reads the exact time each picture shows, seeks to it and compares pixels. Measured with engine 0.1.1: 0 of 2,073,600 pixels differ at all four moments. (The first measurement, with 0.1.0, was 0 at the card entrance, up to 6 levels on the crossfade and 225 edge pixels on the camera move; the residue was the compositor drawing held transitions on its own clock, closed in step 5.) Tolerance in the test: 0.05 percent of pixels over 8 levels. Done.
4. Seeded strokes (circle, frame, underline, arrow) that draw on with the voice; characters; the walkthrough layer (pointer, click, type). Engine 0.3.0: `ink`, `pointer`, `click`, `type`, `who`, minor beats, tokens for all of it; the starter uses each once (a figure beside the heading, a circle on the category row, the pointer gliding to the outcome field, a click, a value typed at 12 characters a second). Proof: `ink.test.js` renders the starter twice in two browser sessions, 20 of 20 stills byte for byte identical; the seek test grew from four moments to nine (a figure fading in, a stroke drawing on, the pointer mid-glide, the ring mid-way, typing mid-word) and measures 0 pixels at all nine; the 5a test still shows inline and app-backed identical. Three things found and fixed on the way: a finished transition baked inline shadowed a later class change (a figure that faded in never faded out), so a finished hold is cancelled instead; two animations in flight sat on different time bases because Chrome starts a transition on the firing frame or the next (33 ms after a dropped frame), so playback now sets each new animation's start time to its beat's nominal time; the typing helper looked its field up when the timeline was built, before an app's state had filled the screen, so it looks it up at beat time; and the first stroke tokens reused `--ink`, which is the theme's text colour, turning every heading orange and costing 6 dB of decode PSNR before the contact sheet gave it away, so strokes have their own `--stroke*` tokens. Done.
5. `vkit render`: every frame at 30 fps from the seek, streamed into ffmpeg (H.264, crf 18, 4:2:0, AAC); each `voice/part-N` clip placed at its part offset, a silent track when there are none; `out/<name>.vtt` and `.srt` from the storyboard's sentences and start times; `out/render.json` with the numbers. The MP4 is exactly the sum of the parts long: the clips are the clock, and a beat after the last clip (the starter's closing fade) is not footage. This is the piece that never shipped before. Proof: `npm test` renders the starter with three beep clips and checks that the MP4 is 2040 frames and 68.000 s; that the frame handed to ffmpeg at each of the four seek-test moments equals `vkit frames` there (0 pixels); that the frame decoded back out of the MP4 is the same picture under the codec (45.4 to 48.6 dB PSNR on the Mac's Chrome; a frame off by one measures 26 to 28 dB; the floor is 40); and that the beeps land at 0.300, 24.300 and 50.300 s. Two things found and fixed on the way, both in the kit, never in a video: a screenshot straight after a seek could show the frame before it (the main thread had committed, the compositor had not drawn; about 47,000 pixels off on the crossfade, worse under load), so the render adapter now opens the page 2 px taller, sets a 1 px marker strip on row 0 in the same task as the seek, waits until a screenshot of row 0 shows it, then takes rows 1 to 1080; and a held transition was drawn on the compositor's clock, stepping about every 10 ms with a phase that changed per browser session (up to 17 levels between runs), so engine 0.1.1 bakes every held transition's value inline on the main thread (`docs/ENGINE.md`). Rendering the 68 s starter took 165 s on the Mac (81 ms a frame, one core); each render writes its own time and machine to `out/render.json`. Done.
5a. The app folder (`apps/<family>/<tool>/`: `app.json`, `tokens.css`, `screen.css`, `states/`, `captures/`, `manifest.csv` as the one list of states), `state(id)` in the engine (0.2.0; not `screen`, which is the browser's display object), `vkit new --app family/tool` (the app copied into `rig/app/`, `states.js` written so the page needs no fetch, the inline screen's marked blocks replaced), `vkit app new`, `vkit app add-state`, `vkit app extract`; the example app `examples/apps/example/placeholder`, made by extracting the starter's own screen. The starter stays self-contained; the whiteboard kind needs no flag (the menu's sources item names the three kinds, step 7). Proof: `npm test` makes three videos from the starter, inline, `--app example/placeholder`, and the round trip (extract the inline screen into a new app, make a video on it), and all 16 frames are byte for byte identical across the three. Found on the way: the step 2 render of the starter had a wrong still at 0.5 s (two fades in flight; 78,986 pixels off against real playback), which the step 5 fixes closed without anyone sampling it; engine 0.2.0 also restores `#mock` on every reset so a changed state never leaks into an earlier frame. Done.
6. `vkit check [--quick]`: five groups, each row a rule id with its measurement, threshold and place. Footage: offline (no http(s) in the rig), deterministic (the stills twice in two sessions, byte for byte), seekable (VK, parts, beats, clips against PARTS when `voice/` exists). Seek-correct: playback against seek at one moment per part. Craft, on the teaching layer only: text floors (54 px on text the narration depends on, `data-decor` exempt), 42 characters a line, title safe, weight, all caps, accent share from the pixels, flashing at 10 fps (a flash is a reversal, not a fade), the longest run of identical frames against the tone's limit, sentence length, storyboard against narration part by part, captions against the storyboard, part and video length; narration rate waits for measured clips. Contrast: each text colour against its effective background; each stroke against the pixels it sits on. Fidelity: each app state that cites a capture, rendered alone and compared at 960x540 grey by structural similarity over windows that have structure (flat ground scores 1.0 whatever is done to it), with coverage and a side-by-side image; reported, not judged, until a dozen real captures have been looked at. `rules.json` comes from `video-reference/craft/rules.json` by `vkit sync-reference`, with its commit recorded; the rail and caption-band rows are marked not applicable to the kit's full-frame layout. Proof (`check.test.js`): the starter passes a full check, 24 rows, 18 pass, 3 info, 3 not measured, 0 fail; a copy with four breaks (a 40 px heading, a kicker outside title safe, an https:// url, part 3's beats removed) fails on exactly `text-floor`, `title-safe`, `offline`, `still-run`; a state against a capture of itself scores 1.000 over 8.7 percent structured coverage, against the same capture shifted 12 px and blurred 0.52 (over all windows, flat ground included, the same shift would score 0.91, which is why flat windows are left out). Found by the checker and fixed in the starter and engine: kickers and card text under the floor (marked decoration or raised to 54 px; the explanation card is now 54 px and 620 px wide, engine 0.3.1), a 69-character line (the sub line now wraps at 960 px: 40 characters in Arial, the Mac's face, where 1100 px gave 46; the sandbox's wider face had hidden it), an 18 s hold in part 3 (two recap beats added), narration files that did not match the storyboard (now one sentence per row, 17 in all), and the bright orange highlight at 1.65:1 on the white screen (now a burnt orange at 4.1:1 and 3.7:1). Not measured yet, and said so in the report: camera holds and settles, narration rate until `vkit measure`. Done.
7a. Looks, words and clips. `vkit sync-reference` copies the seven look packs to `looks/<name>.json` plus `looks/index.json`, and the 35 pattern notes to `patterns/index.json` (id, title, group, phrases, effect, needs, note path), each with the source commit; `looks/faces.json` maps the packs' type-family classes to system stacks (no download, no licence taken on; a shipped face needs an OFL row first, C-TYPE-15). `vkit look <name>` writes `rig/look.css` as `--look-*` tokens; `theme.css` reads brand, then look, then default, so no look is identical and a brand wins; engine 0.3.2 reads size, entrance, crossfade, stroke roughness and glow tokens. Four places a pack meets the kit's layout are decided by `rules.json` contrast, not taste, and written into `look.css`: stage text takes the paper colour when ink cannot hold 4.5:1 on the ground (clippings, archive-camera put words on paper); the stroke is the first of ink then accents that holds 3:1 on both the ground and a white screen, else the theme's highlight (lightboard, clippings); a kind colour that cannot hold 3:1 on the card surface falls back to the card's ink; the explanation card over a white screen is the first of paper, ink, ground that holds 3:1 on white, with text and label chosen to hold on it. `vkit narration` holds parts to about 1000 characters and sentences to 30 words, writes `narration/FULL.md`, estimates at 150 wpm. `vkit measure` reads `voice/part-N.*` with ffprobe and writes exact lengths to the `PARTS` line and `video.json.parts`, locking look, tone, patterns and voice. Proof (`look.test.js`, `words.test.js`): all seven looks pass `vkit check --quick` on the starter, contrast included; 22 of 22 stills identical with and without the empty look layer and again after `vkit look none`; 21 of 22 change under doodle; clips of 23.47, 25.91 and 17.62 s measure in exactly, the page's total is 67.00 s and a render is 67.000 s; a missing clip writes nothing; a look change after measure is refused. Found on the way: a white marker strip on a white page stalled every capture (the strip now has a black base); the accent-share count took a kind colour that fell back to ink for an accent and read a dark stage as 100 percent (an accent must differ from ground and ink). Done.
7b. The brand kit and the menu. A brand is one folder, `brands/<name>/brand.json` plus the files it names: five colours (any may be empty), two faces (display and text, system stacks until a licence row), a mark (text and or a square image) and a banner, each placed by when (never, opener, close, both, always, watermark), where (a corner; top or bottom), size, opacity and seconds. `vkit brand <name>` copies the folder into `rig/brand/` and writes `rig/brand.css` as `--brand-*` tokens, which `theme.css` reads before the look; engine 0.4.0 builds `#mark` and `#banner` from the tokens at boot and shows them by minor beats, inside title safe and above the caption band; `always` hides while a recreated screen is up, `watermark` stays; `vkit brand none` empties the file; `vkit brand --check` measures each brand colour against the surface it sits on (ink on ground 4.5:1; primary, secondary and the mark 3:1 on the ground; the highlight 3:1 on the ground, on white and on the recreated screen's own ground; banner ink on its fill 4.5:1). `brands/example` is neutral; `brands/TEMPLATE` the empty shape. `vkit menu` walks the ten items one at a time (Enter keeps, a number picks, `?` explains, `0` types, `S` saves and runs frames, `Q` stops; `--walk` reads the same answers from a pipe), `--show` prints them, `--set item[.field]=value` answers one from a script, `--explain item` lists the options; the items, their words and fixed options are `menu-defaults.json`, the growing lists come from `looks/index.json`, `brands/`, `patterns/index.json` and the apps folders, and a look or brand chosen in the menu runs `vkit look` or `vkit brand`. A video records answers only; items locked by `vkit measure` refuse a change and accept the value they have. Proof (`brand.test.js`, `menu.test.js`): 22 of 22 stills identical with and without the empty brand layer, and identical to engine 0.3.2 on the pushed kit; 21 of 22 change under the example brand, which passes `vkit check --quick` on the starter, contrast measured on the pixels; the mark is up at 2 s and in the close, down at 12 and 30 s, 40 px high at y 54 inside title safe, and the banner spans the frame with its bottom at 880; `always` reads opacity 0 over the screen and `watermark` 0.9; `none` restores every still; a highlight of `#ff7b54` fails on white and on the screen and nothing else; ten menu items with 8 look, 2 brand, 35 pattern and 1 app options from the files; the menu's `look.css` and `brand.css` equal `vkit look`'s and `vkit brand`'s byte for byte; after measure, four items show locked, a locked change is refused, the same value accepted, the brand still changes; a piped walk sets job.kind, a typed length, a look that is applied and two patterns and stops at `Q`. Found on the way: the checker caught the mark at 40 px under the read-text floor (the mark is decoration now), its line box 4 px above title safe (line-height 1), and a teal that held 3.3:1 on white but 2.96:1 on the placeholder screen's lavender ground, so `--check` now tests the screen's own ground too. Done.
8. The plugin: skills over `vkit`, guardrails. Drafted 1 Oct 2026 in `packages/plugin/` (six skills, the guard hook with 21 passing cases); installs and is tried on a real video once the commands it calls exist.
9. `vkit capture`: the agent walks a task in a browser and writes tagged screenshots and the manifest, naming states as it shoots them; a skill turns a capture into a state fragment with the app's tokens.
10. The sample video (an AWS console task I can capture freely), which is also the first real app, in the first private family repo (`video-apps-aws`); the README for a reader who has two minutes.
11. The MCP server; cloud render and a voice provider as adapters when they earn their place. When they land, each adapter prints the cost of a batch and stops for a yes, and the guard hook's `--approved` rule goes; the rule itself (no paid call without a recorded go-ahead) stays.
12. The twin runner: the same app folder plus `flows.json`, a learner clicks through, every unbuilt control shows the one practice notice. Its own proof. Generic runner, private content. `docs/TWIN-CARRYOVER.md` holds what the earlier twin work taught.

Order after step 5: 5a, then 4 (strokes and the pointer target elements inside states, so the state format comes first), then 6 onward.
