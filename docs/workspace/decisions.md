# Decisions

One line each, with the reason, so they are not re-argued. Add to the end; a line a later decision replaces gets a note in brackets, never a rewrite. (A few 7 and 8 October lines sit out of date order; they were added where the hand was.)

- 2026-10-01: Two repos, `video-kit` (product) and `video-reference` (research), side by side under `~/Developer/video/`, public, MIT; no org, no project board, no shared-tooling repo. For one person the shared rulebook moves inside the kit.
- 2026-10-01: Plain JavaScript, Node 18+, npm workspaces, no build step. Playwright with the installed Chrome (`PW_CHANNEL=chrome`); nothing downloads a browser.
- 2026-10-01: The command is `vkit` (was `vk` for a day). The page namespace stays `window.VK`.
- 2026-10-01: A video is a folder made by `vkit new`; the engine is copied in at its version (`rig/engine/VERSION`) so a video opens from disk and never changes under its owner. `vkit upgrade` moves it.
- 2026-10-01: Everything is a function in `packages/core`; the CLI, the plugin, the MCP server and any app are thin shells. Four adapters (voice, render, script, hosting), one file each, local implementations first.
- 2026-10-01: One layout: the screen at native pixels fills the frame, the explanation is a card that places itself beside the element it names, the camera is clamped to the frame and never under scale 1. The older inset-with-a-rail layout is not carried.
- 2026-10-01: Looks, patterns, rules and brands are data files made from the reference by `vkit sync-reference`; the menu reads them; no list is typed into a skill.
- 2026-10-02: The research moved into `video-reference` as it stood (seven creator studies with the deep pass, 96 craft rules, 35 patterns, seven look packs, six library studies, licences), with product and company names removed and paths pointed at `video-kit`; it is Matthew's own work, written with him, about public videos and standards. The earlier plan to rewrite it note by note was dropped as wasted effort.
- 2026-10-01: AWS enters as adapters (cloud render, Polly) when they earn their place; no app, database or auth before a non-terminal user exists.
- 2026-10-01: Matthew keeps working with the current chat until the product is more mature; a dedicated Claude project comes later, seeded from `claude/new-project-instructions.md`.
- 2026-10-02: Recreated apps are their own folders, not part of any video and not in the kit: `~/Developer/video/apps/<family>/<tool>/` with `app.json`, `tokens.css`, `states/` (one HTML fragment per captured state), `captures/` and `manifest.csv`. One app is what a learner would call "the tool" (per AWS service, plus `console` and `cli`); a state is one screen in one condition; ten to thirty states per app, split by area past that. A video names its app in `video.json` and the states it uses are copied in at build time, like the engine, so a finished video never changes when the app moves on.
- 2026-10-02: App repos are private, one per app family (`video-apps-aws`, later `video-apps-langchain`), each cloned into `apps/<family>/`; nothing from one family sits in another's repo. The kit only ever looks for `apps/<family>/<tool>/app.json`, so splitting an app into its own repo later costs the kit nothing. `video-kit` and `video-reference` stay public; the apps and the twins are the method and stay private.
- 2026-10-02: Three kinds of video, and the engine does not care which: whiteboard or theory (no screen), one-off screen (built inline from a couple of captures, as the starter does), app-backed (`vkit new --app aws/bedrock`). `vkit app extract` lifts an inline screen into an app the day it is needed twice; nobody builds an app ahead of need. The starter stays self-contained so `vkit new` works from a bare clone; the app-backed path has its own small example app inside the kit. (Superseded in part on 2026-10-08: an app is built before or without any video; the three kinds stand.)
- 2026-10-02: Recreate from your own captures, cite each, never invent a control. That is the rule for every recreated screen; there is no rule against recreating a product. The separate rule that nothing is copied from an employer's or client's repository stands; it is about files, not screens.
- 2026-10-02: Fidelity is a measurement: `vkit check` renders each state and compares it with its capture at the same size, a structural similarity at reduced size and a side-by-side image. The image is what a person judges; the number is the trend; the threshold is set after looking at a dozen, not asserted first.
- 2026-10-02: The paid-call rule stays (no paid service without a recorded go-ahead; Matthew wants to see it and approve, not be blocked). The mechanism changes when the adapters land: each adapter prints the cost of a batch and stops for a yes; the guard hook's `--approved` rule goes at that point, not before.
- 2026-10-02: The twin (a learner clicks through the same app states, `flows.json` says which click shows which state, every unbuilt control shows one notice) is its own roadmap step, last, after the app folder exists. Its runner is generic and may live in the public kit; every twin's content is private. (Superseded on 2026-10-10: the light twin comes next, before looks and brands; the full runner stays last.)
- 2026-10-02: Roadmap order after the renderer: 5a app folder and `screen()`, then 4 strokes and the pointer (they target elements inside states, so the state format comes first), then 6 checker with fidelity, 7, 8, 9, 10 (the sample video is also the first real app), 11, 12 twin.
- 2026-10-02: Proofs run on the Mac's Chrome; quick checks may run anywhere with a Chromium. `npm test` runs its files one at a time because the playback proof is a real-time measurement.
- 2026-10-02: `manifest.csv` is the one list of an app's states (the design note had it in `app.json` too); people edit the CSV, so it wins. An app has `tokens.css` (palette and faces) and `screen.css` (structure), both scoped to `#mock`: the structure is as much the recreated product as the colours, and one file beats copying it into every state.
- 2026-10-02: The engine call is `state(id)`, not `screen(id)`: `window.screen` is the browser's display object and a page global with that name is a trap.
- 2026-10-02: The checker's numbers are copied, never retyped: `vkit sync-reference` copies `video-reference/craft/rules.json` into the kit with its commit; the rail and caption-band rows are marked not applicable because the kit has one full-frame layout and captions as a sidecar. A rule the checker cannot measure yet is reported as not measured with where the number lives; a silent pass is a lie.
- 2026-10-02: Fidelity is structural similarity at 960x540 grey over windows that have structure (variance at least 4), with the coverage reported; flat ground would otherwise score 1.0 whatever was done to it (a 12 px shift with blur measured 0.91 over all windows and 0.52 over structured ones). Reported, not judged, until a dozen real captures have been looked at.
- 2026-10-02: A flash is a reversal: a pair of opposing luminance changes of 10 percent or more (WCAG 2.3.1); a fade in one direction over several frames is one transition. The first count took a 0.6 s fade to a white screen for four flashes.
- 2026-10-02: The starter's highlight is a burnt orange (`#d4561e`, 4.1:1 on the stage and 3.7:1 on a white screen) because a stroke has to hold 3:1 on whatever it sits on; the brighter orange measured 1.65:1 on the screen. Read text in the starter sits at 54 px or is marked `data-decor`; the explanation card's body is 54 px.
- 2026-10-02: A look pack is applied as tokens (`rig/look.css`, `--look-*`) that `theme.css` reads after the brand and before its defaults; no look renders identically and a brand wins. Where a pack's colours meet the kit's layout, the contrast rules decide, not taste: stage text takes the paper colour when ink cannot hold 4.5:1 on the ground; the stroke is the first of ink then accents that holds 3:1 on both the ground and a white screen; a kind colour under 3:1 on the card falls back to the card's ink; the explanation card over a white screen is the first of paper, ink, ground that holds 3:1 on white. Each choice is written into `look.css` as a comment.
- 2026-10-02: Type families stay classes in the packs and map to system stacks in `looks/faces.json`; no face is downloaded or shipped until it has a licence row (OFL or an explicit web or app licence). A render is therefore pixel-identical per machine, not across machines, which the proofs already assume.
- 2026-10-02: `vkit measure` makes the clips the clock: exact lengths to two decimals into the `PARTS` line and `video.json.parts`; look, tone, patterns and voice lock from then on because each would re-time the video; `vkit look` refuses a change while locked. A missing clip writes nothing.
- 2026-10-02: An accent, for the accent-share rule, is a colour that differs from both the ground and the text colour by more than the match distance; a kind colour that fell back to ink is not one.
- 2026-10-02: A brand is applied as tokens too (`rig/brand.css`, `--brand-*`, read before the look), and the mark and the banner are tokens the engine turns into elements and minor beats at boot, so no brand means no elements and a byte-identical render. Their placement (a corner inside title safe, a bottom edge above the caption band) is the engine's and the rules', not the brand's; `always` hides over a recreated screen so the product is never covered, `watermark` is the one that stays. A brand's colours are written as given and measured, never changed: `vkit brand --check` reports each against the surface it sits on, including the recreated screen's own ground, because a teal that held 3.3:1 on white measured 2.96:1 on the placeholder screen's lavender.
- 2026-10-02: The kit ships a neutral `brands/example` and an empty `brands/TEMPLATE`; Matthew's own brand (swab.the.stack) is not developed yet and will live outside the public kit when it is.
- 2026-10-02: `vkit menu` is the one front door to `video.json.menu`. The items, their words and their fixed options are `menu-defaults.json`; the growing lists are read from `looks/index.json`, `brands/`, `patterns/index.json` and the apps folders; a video records answers only (value, from, chosen_on, note). Choosing a look or a brand in the menu runs `vkit look` or `vkit brand`, so the record and the rig cannot disagree. Layout offers `full` only and says the classic inset layout is not built; voice offers only what the renderer does (clips or none, sidecar captions or none, no music bed).
- 2026-10-02: The text of a brand mark or banner is decoration (`data-decor`): the voice never depends on it, so the 54 px read-text floor does not apply, but title safe and 4.5:1 still do.
- 2026-10-02: Progress is a core contract, not a print: every slow function takes `opts.progress(step, done, total, note)` and the shell draws it (the CLI: a bar that rewrites one line on a terminal, plain lines every five seconds in a pipe, `VKIT_QUIET=1` for none). The proofs report every 20 seconds. The MCP server and the cloud renderer will read the same calls; nothing in core knows which shell is listening.
- 2026-10-02: A skill never carries a list the kit can print: the plugin's typed list of menu items is out of the repo (kept on Matthew's Desktop), and `video-menu` reads `vkit menu --explain` and writes `vkit menu --set`. `plugin.test.js` holds every skill to the commands that exist: a `vkit` command, a flag or a menu item named in a skill that the CLI does not have fails the suite.
- 2026-10-05: A surface that read text sits on is opaque when anything sits behind it. The explanation card's 93 percent panel let the screen's values show under its words, and the contrast rule could not see it because it measures the surface, not what shows through; `surface-opaque` now fails that by element, alpha and what is behind. The concept cards' 3 percent fill stays: nothing sits behind them but the stage.
- 2026-10-05: The explanation card keeps clear of the named element's neighbours (its siblings, and every leaf element with an id in the recreated screen), not only of the element itself: the sides of the element first, then the sides of the group, then the side that covers least; an author's `side` still wins. Found by the frames skill looking at stills one by one, which the contact sheets had not shown; `docs/FINDINGS.md` holds what the frames show and the checker does not, until each is fixed.
- 2026-10-05: A camera frame over a recreated screen starts at a chrome edge (past the side menu, below the top bar) or shows the chrome whole; a cut label is a framing fault even when no rule measures it. The starter's part 2 offsets are measured from `pos()`, not guessed.
- 2026-10-05: A proof that compares two outputs passes when both are wrong the same way. The seek-equals-playback proof held at 0 pixels for four steps while no stroke ever drew in a rendered frame, because the seek's bake and the live transition failed alike; a person watching the MP4 saw it in a minute. Every step's landing now includes someone watching the output, and a fault seen that way goes into `docs/FINDINGS.md` like any other.
- 2026-10-05: Anything that looks hand drawn is drawn: a stroke that appears whole is a fault, not a style. The draw time is the look's (`--stroke-draw`, 0.6 s in the house theme); whether that reads as drawing at the viewer's pace is judged on the sample video.
- 2026-10-05: A stroke is measured against each surface it sits on, never against an average of them: the pixels beside each drawn stroke pixel, bucketed by colour, the worst surface with 8 percent or more of them. An average of a paper card and a dark ground is a grey the stroke never touches; it read 2.93:1 for a stroke holding 3.6:1 and 4.5:1 on the real surfaces.
- 2026-10-05: The capture is a walkthrough document, not a folder of pictures: one `.docx` with a heading per step, a line saying what was done, the screenshot under it and a note for what a still cannot carry. The document carries the order and the words a folder cannot, it is what a person with the tool naturally writes, and it is read with its pictures in place. The agent never captures a product screen itself; the brief (`docs/CAPTURE.md`) goes to whoever has the tool. The same brief serves a one-off screen and a recreated app. (Superseded on 2026-10-08, later: the agent captures with the kit's own browser, `vkit shoot`, with the person signed in; the document path stays for a capturer who is not at that machine.)
- 2026-10-05: `vkit capture` checks the shape of what arrived and never its content: PNG; 1920x1080, or 3840x2160 as a 2x capture, any other 16:9 at or above 1920 wide a warning, anything else a fail; no two pictures the same; every picture under a heading with a line before it. A green run says the files are usable, not that the screens are right or clean; the pictures are opened and read, every one, by the `video-capture` skill or a person. Pixels are never touched; a 2x capture stays 2x and is recorded.
- 2026-10-05: No browser is a plain stop, not a hang and not a download: with no `PW_CHANNEL` and no bundled Chromium, every browser command fails in under a second with `no browser: set PW_CHANNEL=chrome`, before Playwright can suggest `playwright install`, which the guard would block and an agent might loop on.
- 2026-10-05: For a web or browser-backed app, the capturer reads the page's own values (computed colours as hex, the font stack and sizes, region sizes, exact labels) and writes them under the picture in the walkthrough document, so the recreated screen starts from measurements rather than from a picture read by eye. Numbers and words only: the product's HTML, CSS, JavaScript, icons and images are never pasted into the document or any repository; the screen is recreated, never copied.
- 2026-10-06: The privilege boundary for a capture is the account, not the policy. A console page is drawn by a long tail of read calls, so a tight permission set draws Access Denied panels into training pictures and cannot trim a bucket list at all; an account made for capturing, with nothing in it, under AdministratorAccess with a one-hour session, bounds the blast radius and keeps the pictures honest. Found when the AWS side showed a CDK bootstrap bucket carrying the account id in its name in the account first proposed; an empty list is a fix, a mask on a row is not.
- 2026-10-06: Two ways to a capture set, one folder. The kit's own capture browser (`vkit shoot`, an agent driving it through the Playwright MCP, the person signing in once in a visible window) is the first choice whenever the tool can be reached from the Mac that runs the kit; the walkthrough document (`vkit capture`) is for a person or chat that has only the screen. Both write `captures/CAP-NNN.png`, `walkthrough.md` and `captures.csv`; the shoot path adds `CAP-NNN.json` with the page's own values. The earlier plan of a human at the browser typing step text was a gap chosen rather than engineered, and is withdrawn.
- 2026-10-06: Private text is caught before a picture exists, not blurred after. `vkit shoot` hides the masked elements (layout kept, no pixel edited), sweeps the visible text for a 12-digit number, an ARN, an email address, an IP address and the listed literals, and refuses to write while any remain, naming the element so the fix is a mask and a new shot. The sweep reads text: nothing drawn on a canvas or baked into an image. A reader still opens every picture.
- 2026-10-06: The capture browser's profile is launched with Chrome's mock keychain on every launch, never mixed with the real one (cookies written under one key are unreadable under the other, and a headed sign-in followed by a headless relaunch would come up signed out and look like a browser fault). At rest the profile folder (mode 700, outside every repo) is protected by its permissions and the session's own length, not by the Keychain; that is acceptable only because the capture account is empty and its session is an hour. `vkit shoot stop` asks the browser to close itself over CDP before any signal: a signal is a crash to Chrome and cookies not yet flushed are lost with it.
- 2026-10-06: A capture session's clock starts at sign-in, not at the first picture (PT1H on the capture permission set). The skill reads the session's age from `vkit shoot status` and reports a signed-out relaunch as the clock running out or a missing sign-in, never as headless failing.
- 2026-10-06: MCP here means a local process, never a service. The Playwright MCP server (Microsoft, the same team as the Playwright the kit has used since step 2) runs on the Mac and speaks to Claude Code over standard input and output; the only network it touches is the page the browser visits. It is pinned in the kit's dev dependencies and lockfile rather than fetched by `npx` at run time, registered for the video folder only (`--scope local`), and fenced to a tool's origins once a first capture has shown which hosts the tool uses. What leaves the Mac is what the agent reads (page text, pictures it opens, command output), to Anthropic's model, the same channel every Claude Code session already uses; which is one more reason the capture account is empty. The kit's own MCP server (step 11) follows the same shape: local, standard input and output, never hosted.
- 2026-10-06: The product's typeface is recreated as a face of our own, never shipped as its file. `vkit face` draws each glyph of the face the page is using on a canvas, traces the outlines, measures widths and kerning and writes an OTF with a provenance note; nothing is read from the product's font file, so there is no file to license or credit, and the result is ours to reuse. Replicating a painting is allowed so long as it is not credited as an original; the face is credited as a tracing of the rendered glyphs and used only inside recreated screens.
- 2026-10-06: Logos and icons stay in the captures and in the recreation. The video is a screen recording in stills, and a console without its logo is not the console; nothing is cropped out of a capture, and the recreation's pictures are copies of regions of our own captures (`vkit app crop`, `crops/crops.csv`), never files from the product. Text is never a picture. The learning twin's own treatment of marks is a later decision taken with the twin, in a preamble notice as before, and does not shape the app folders.
- 2026-10-06: Every recreated value is read from a capture and cited, and what could not be read is written down: `fidelity.md` in the app folder has one row per colour, face, part, picture and text value with its source and `true`, `substituted`, `invented` or `missing`. Fidelity is judged by frames beside the capture (`vkit check`'s fidelity row and `out/fidelity/`), never by reading the code. A masked label, a user name and a real timestamp are invented stand-ins and say so; a behaviour no capture shows waits for its capture and is never invented.
- 2026-10-06: Anything that follows a text runs after it, never at a measured x: an Info after a heading, the arrow after a link, a caret after a label, the next crumb after a chevron. The gap is measured; the text's own width carries the rest, so the layout holds in the fallback face and lands exactly in the true one. The check judges this (`overlaps`, `overflow`, `faces` rows) because a similarity score cannot see a link sitting on its heading and a person can.
- 2026-10-06: A beat that names something not on screen is recorded, not thrown and not swallowed. Engine 0.4.3 routes every lookup a beat makes through one function that records the miss in `VK.faults()` with the beat's time and does nothing else; `vkit check` names each in `beats-on-screen`. A crash stops every render over one id, and a silent skip hides a fault no check measures; a named row does neither.
- 2026-10-07: Three roles make a video and none skips the other two: the producer owns the goal, the words and the first watch; the subject expert owns the teaching (an outline, the storyboard rows in substance, the pickups, the fact check of the finished narration); the director owns the words under the craft rules, coverage against the app's states, the camera and the timeline. The expert is the technical advisor, not the screenwriter, because the script has to obey rules the expert does not know.
- 2026-10-07: The storyboard and the capture walkthrough are two documents with two owners. The walkthrough is evidence for the app engine: coverage of the tool in the order the tool presents it, in the app repo. The storyboard is the teaching: which moments, in what order, what is said over each, in the video folder. They overlap on a linear task and still differ; a gap in the storyboard (a dropdown no state has) is a pickup request back to the expert and a short capture session, never an invention.
- 2026-10-07: Every handoff between the roles is a skill and a file, never a typed prompt: `vkit brief` writes the request with the app's states in it, the expert's chat answers with `video-brief` in the kit's own storyboard table, the director's `video-script` lands and gates it, the expert's `video-fact-check` returns a verdict per sentence, and `vkit check` proves the rows (`sentences-match`, `storyboard-covered`). Procedures repeat; prompts drift.
- 2026-10-07: Script first, then the producer's own recording, then the timeline timed to the spoken word, then footage. Nothing is rendered until the words are mostly settled and recorded; beats are placed at the moment a word is said (Whisper word times on the recording, the same local tool as the meeting transcripts) rather than at an estimated offset. The order of the earlier work was forced the other way round by generated voice, and the pacing was fixed afterwards; here the clips are the clock from the start and a re-recorded part re-times only itself.
- 2026-10-08, later: The capture runs where the browser is. The expert's chat is a cloud session and the capture browser only ever runs on the person's machine, so `capture-walkthrough` runs in a Claude Code session there (the director's side) against the request the expert wrote; `vkit handoffs` shows an app's open request as the director's. Also from the first fact check: `vkit handoffs` reads the verdict column of `fact-check.md` (anything but `true` sends the video back to the director; rows newer than the narration do the same), and a check is current, and rows are unchanged, when their sentences are the narration's sentences, not when a file is younger (the third pass checked words after the brief changed, and dates alone sent the video round once more for nothing), the expert fixes a drift of its own making (row order, a wrong state) in the same pass, and the fact check opens the captures the rows cite, because a count copied from the capture request into the manifest, the row and the sentence was wrong in all four and right only in the picture.
- 2026-10-08: The idea originates with the subject expert, and the apps are their own assets. A video starts in the expert's chat as `brief.md` and the screens it needs; when no app exists the expert writes the tool's coverage as `apps/<family>/<tool>/capture-request.md` and runs the capture (`capture-walkthrough`, the person signed in), and the director's chat recreates from the captures. An app is built before or without any video, versioned, and taken into a video by `vkit new --app` or `vkit app use` (into a video that exists, or the copy brought up to date after a re-capture); a video holds one app. The states handshake (`vkit brief`, `brief-request.md`) is the expert's second touch, not the first. `vkit handoffs` covers app folders too (coverage waits for captures, captures wait for states). Raised by Matthew the same morning: the SME chat has the pulse on gaps and topics, so the order has to start there.
- 2026-10-08: The video folder is the handoff and no text travels through the producer. Every request and answer between the roles is a file in `videos/<name>/` (`brief-request.md` answered by `brief.md`, the storyboard rows and `capture-request.md`; `narration/FULL.md` answered by `fact-check.md`; `voice/part-N.m4a` the recording), the newer side of a pair is the open side, and `vkit handoffs` says whose turn it is. Both chats are linked to the same drive, so the expert's skills find the waiting video and write beside it; the producer's messages are about the teaching, never about routing. Pasting between chats is the fallback when a chat cannot reach the folder, not the way. Raised by Matthew on the first real run, when the first step of the workflow was him carrying the request by hand.
- 2026-10-07: The capture browser only ever means the Chrome already on the machine, so `vkit shoot` and `vkit face` assume it without `PW_CHANNEL`; frames, render and check keep asking, because they can run on a bundled browser where one exists.
- 2026-10-09: The card keeps clear of everything the viewer is reading, named or not. The first frames with cards over the real console put them on its text, because the placer avoided elements with ids and a console's text has none. Engine 0.4.4 treats every visible leaf of the recreated screen as an obstacle, searches the frame for the nearest clear spot when no side of the element is clear, waits for a camera move to land before a card arrives, and cuts a swapped card instead of sliding it; `vkit check` proves it per still in `cards-clear`. A card's words are the director's cut of the expert's Card cell to two lines of 54 px (about 40 characters, 20 a line). A row whose screen has no clear spot at any scale the row allows gets no card and the sentence carries it, with a stroke on the control where the hold would otherwise run past the tone's limit; whether the card should make its own room is F5, step 10d. (Superseded on 2026-10-09, late: over a recreated screen a note replaces the card; the card belongs to concept scenes.)
- 2026-10-09: A picture is drawn in whole screen pixels. A crop from the 2x capture drawn at a fractional CSS size (18 by 21.5) rasterizes differently between browser sessions and breaks the deterministic proof one run in three (F9). The next change to `vkit app crop` rounds a region up to even width and height at 2x; the S3 app's odd crops are re-cut then. Text keeps its measured half-pixel positions.
- 2026-10-09, evening: A console video is a screen recording in stills, not photographs with a camera over them. The first watch of the S3 video read as a slideshow: every row was a whole-screen replacement and the timeline used no pointer, click, typing or scroll. From now: a recreated screen is a page, and its states morph in place and scroll (engine 0.5.0); the storyboard carries an Action per row (what the learner does, in the console's words) and On screen is the console's answer; the director animates every action, the cursor first and the screen after; the console sits at 1:1 and the camera pushes in only to read; a page change is a cut, as it is in the console; a field never goes from empty to full without the typing on screen (`typeTo`), a page never scrolls without the scroll, a page never changes without the click. `vkit check` holds it with `actions-move`. The proofs that passed before measured stills, and a still cannot show that nothing moved: the watch is a proof the checks do not replace. Lessons taken from an earlier project's animations in their own words; nothing copied.
- 2026-10-09, evening: The deterministic proof holds the seek proof's tolerance. Two browser sessions rasterize an anti-aliased curve under a fractional camera scale a few levels apart (12 pixels by 17 on a button's rounded end mid move), and no change of ours makes that identical. The row passes under 0.05 percent of pixels over 8 levels, as `seek-equals-playback` always has, and names every still that differed and by how much; pictures are drawn in whole screen pixels so the one cause that was ours is gone (F9 closed).
- 2026-10-09, late: The moment between the cause and the answer is part of the screen layer. A click presses the control (the app's pressed look from a capture; a fixed darkening, written as substituted, until then); a page change shows the load the browser shows (the chrome stays, the content goes, the new page comes 450 ms later); nothing on a recreated screen changes in an instant cut except a page's own render. The explanation card does not sit over a console: a note does, one line, decoration, with a leader the look draws; the card's kicker is gone and the card belongs to concept scenes. Hover and pressed looks are captured as part of the layer one click out from the path.
- 2026-10-10: Over a recreated screen the click has no ring; the control's press is the click. A note's spot is chosen so its leader crosses nothing, before nearness. Placement during a scroll reads the destination. The third watch named all three.
- 2026-10-10, afternoon: The proof of concept stands. The fourth watch of `create-s3-bucket` read as the console: pointer, click, press, load, typing, scroll, notes with leaders, the banner framed. What remains is not mechanics: the looks and brands over the teaching layer, concept scenes for theory, and other surfaces (the command line, code) as their own app kinds.
