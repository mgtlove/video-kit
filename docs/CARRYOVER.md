# Carryover: what the earlier work taught, so none of it is lost

The kit is rebuilt clean, not copied. This file lists what the rebuild has to honour, by concept, so a step is not finished until it does. Facts, numbers and lessons; no code.

## Contracts

- A still frame at any second equals playback at that second. Replay old beats snapped, re-fire in-flight beats with motion on, hold each animation at `t minus its beat time`; measured at 0 of 2,073,600 pixels different, about 4 ms a frame.
- Beats are replayable from a reset; animations fill forwards; no timers; nothing depends on wall-clock time.
- Measure positions through the offset chain, never the bounding rectangle, so the camera transform does not poison a measurement.
- Doodles are built after fonts are ready and after dynamic content is filled, or they measure the wrong size.
- Stroke geometry is seeded, so a re-render is identical; change the element, never the seed.
- A part is one voice clip, at most about 1000 characters, so it is cheap to regenerate; part lengths are the exact clip lengths (padding compounded into seconds of drift when parts were placed end to end).
- The page scales to the window; a take is only 1:1 when the page is 1920x1080, and the pre-flight line must say the scale.
- A deliberate decision: reduced-motion preferences are ignored by the rig, because the rig is footage, not a page a person uses.

## Layout and camera

- Two layouts: classic (screen inset beside a rail, a caption band kept clear) and full (screen at 1920x1080 at native pixels, concept text large, the explanation card placed beside the element it names, re-placed on every camera move; the camera keeps the subject above the caption line and never shows the stage at the screen's top or sides).
- Review finding that produced the full layout: text slides read as empty; the product screen read as three quarters of the frame.
- Camera over a still: about 3 percent of frame height per second, 5 to 8 percent scale over a hold, the reveal timed to the last word; only the evidence layer moves, text stays still.

## Craft numbers (sourced in video-reference/craft)

- Title safe 96 px sides, 54 px top and bottom at 1080p (SMPTE ST 2046-1); action safe 67/38 (EBU R95).
- Text floors 72 px for anything the narration depends on, 54 px minimum; 42 characters a line; captions about 72 px, white on black, bottom of the frame.
- Contrast 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for strokes and large text, 7:1 for the strict level (WCAG); no flash faster than 3 a second; meaning never carried by colour alone.
- Accent share at most 10 percent of the frame; three accents at most.
- Narration 140 to 170 words a minute (most studied creators run 200 to 220 by cutting the gaps, not by speaking faster); a still without a build or a moving camera no longer than 8 s (12 s formal); videos under about five minutes.

## Brand and tokens

- Two scopes, never merged: the teaching layer (ours, restyles freely) and the recreated product screen (measured from the real thing, never restyled, because a learner has to recognise it).
- A brand is one folder: five colours, two faces, a mark and a banner with when (never, opener, close, both, always, watermark), where (a corner, inside title safe and above the caption band), size and opacity; applied through tokens with fallbacks so "no brand" renders identically.
- Build broadly, restrain per audience: capability stays in the rig; a formal audience gets less through tokens, copy and the timeline.

## Research shape (video-reference)

- Seven creator studies, each with a shot-by-shot log (relation codes: literal, illustration, evidence, pun, counterpoint, reaction, text, ident, demo, ambient, drawing, clipping, artefact, landscape, witness, map), a rhetoric map and measured audio (loudness distribution, pauses, bed, timbre); nothing of theirs copied; credited.
- 35 patterns, one file each: where observed with times, the numbers, the effect, a required counter-example, what a rig would need; indexed by the phrase a person would use.
- Seven look packs with one key set; six runnable library studies (anime.js, Motion, three.js, lottie-web, Web Animations seek, CSS 3D) with licences recorded.
- Method for studying a video inside a browser tab, silently: frame differencing on a small canvas for cuts, montage sheets with captions under each shot, a Web Audio graph ending in a gain of zero sampled from the audio thread.

## Process lessons

- Fetch and read the day's commits in sibling repos before building in a shared one; two people solved the same feedback within an hour once.
- Never delete; move aside and say so. Never download a browser by surprise. Never call a paid API without a recorded go-ahead. State failure modes before anything irreversible.
- Docs change in the same commit as the code; a change that only lives in code has not landed.
- A person judges the rig by looking at rendered frames, never by reading code.
- Verification before the next step: byte-compare frames before and after a change that should not change them.
- A proof that compares two outputs passes when both are wrong the same way: the seek proof matched playback at 0 pixels for four steps while no stroke drew in any rendered frame. Watching the finished video is a proof the suite cannot replace, and it goes on every step's landing.
- The order of operations on an earlier project was forced the wrong way round: the AI audio had to be generated first, and only then could the pacing be made exact and the visuals tweaked to it. Here the script is settled first, the narrator records, and the visuals are timed to the spoken word; the clips are the clock from the start.
- A session's sandbox is one disk shared by every branch of the conversation that uses it. When a message is edited or sent again, the earlier branch's file changes stay on disk while its words leave the context, so an agent can meet its own work as a stranger's (7 October 2026: the brief command and three skills, written and then rediscovered twenty minutes later). The check is the session transcript on disk, not memory; and nothing reaches the person's machine or GitHub until it has been read against the plan and proved like anything else.
- A command is proved by running the command, not only the function under it. `vkit brief` shipped green with a name the CLI did not have in scope (`pos` for `args`), because its test called `core.briefRequest` and the command line was never run; the first real run died on it (8 October 2026). A test for a command spawns `bin/vkit.js` at least once, the way the person will type it.
- A check that filters its input silently is a check that passes on the wrong thing. `storyboard-covered` read the storyboard through a reader that dropped every row in a part the rig did not have yet (the starter has three parts; the expert wrote four), so it passed on 11 of 15 rows and said 11 with no fail. Seen on the first real storyboard (8 October 2026). Coverage now reads every row, and `sentences-match` names the rows that sit in a part the rig lacks instead of losing them. A reader that skips rows must say what it skipped.
- Words copy; pictures do not. "All four boxes checked" went from the 6 October capture request into the manifest note, the expert's row and the director's sentence, each written from the one before, and was wrong in all four: the picture shows one control and four it governs, and the recreation, made from the picture, had it right all along. Every stage that writes a label a person reads checks it against the capture, and the fact check opens the pictures the rows cite (8 October 2026).
- Never assume a chat has a skill, or the current one. The skills reach a chat only as the uploaded `vkit` plugin on the account (claude.ai: Customize, Plugins, Upload a plugin); a chat started before an upload keeps the old version; a skill proposed from a chat may never have been saved; a cloud chat cannot run what needs the machine (the capture browser). On 8 October 2026 three chats and a person spent an afternoon on assumptions about who had which skill in which state. Before relying on a skill in another chat, have that chat list its skills and version; every kit change to a skill bumps `plugin.json` and says in the delivery whether a re-upload is due.
- The obstacles are what the viewer reads, not what the code named. A placer that only avoided elements with ids put every card on the console's text, because a console's text has no ids. Measure against every visible leaf.
- Count the camera's move. A card placed for where the camera is going, shown while the camera is still going, sits on whatever slides under it; the still at 0.3 s into a travel proved it. The engine waits for the move to land, and a check sample taken mid move must know a waiting card is not up.
- One style flush cannot show two changes. A card taken down and put up in the same beat never went down: the browser saw on then on. A swap needs a flush between (transitions off, class off, flush, transitions on, class on).
- A recreated screen is one layer. Its own z-indexes (page under top bar) leaked into the stage's stacking once the screen reached full opacity and painted over the strokes drawn on it; `isolation: isolate` on the screen keeps them inside. Seen as a stroke that was in the DOM, computed as drawn, and not on the frame.
- Pictures in whole pixels. A crop drawn at 21.5 px flaked the deterministic row one run in three; at 22 px, one run in seven and a level on a border corner. The proof names the still now, so the next flake is chased, not re-run.
- A still cannot show that nothing moved. Every proof passed on a video that read as a slideshow, because every proof measured stills; the first watch found it in a minute. A screen-recording video is proved by motion: a row that acts must change the frame inside its beat (`actions-move`), and the person's watch stays the proof the checks do not replace.
- The engine had the pointer, the click, the typing and the scroll since step 4; the first real video used none. A capability that the storyboard has no column for does not get used. The Action column exists so the director cannot write a slideshow by translating rows.
- A recreated screen is a page, not a stack of photographs. Whole-screen replacement made a scroll, a typed name and an error line into cuts; states of one screen must change in place (morph) and scroll by a transition. The captures are evidence for one page at several positions and in several states, and the generator already drew it that way; only the delivery was snapshots.
- Nothing appears without its cause on screen. The cursor goes there, the click lands, the characters are typed, the page scrolls, then the screen answers; a page change is the one honest cut. A field that fills in a cut breaks the illusion at once (the producer's words).
- The card's size came from applying the read-text floor to text nobody has to read. The voice carries the words; the card repeats them; by the kit's own definition that is decoration. A rule applied to the wrong thing produced a huge, correct card. Ask what a thing is before applying the floor.
- A click with no answer from the control reads as a photograph being clicked; a page that appears in one frame reads as a slide. The press is two frames and the load is half a second, and they are the difference the viewer feels but cannot name.
- Place for where things will be, not where they are. A note placed while the page was scrolling pointed at where its element had been; the leader ran off the frame. Anything placed during a move reads the move's destination.
- A teaching device beside a real control competes with it. The click ring over the console made the button's own press invisible; over a recreated screen the press is the click.
- `video.json` is written by `vkit app use`, never delivered by hand: it carries the app's path on that machine, and a copy from another machine sends the checker looking for captures that are not there (10 October 2026). Deliver the rig, the engine and the storyboard; let the command write the record.
- A doc that names a thing the code does not have is a bug, and a proof catches it the way a proof catches any bug. The hardening audit of 10 October 2026 found eleven wrong names (a command that never existed, a flag the kit never set, a key the kit never read, a step number, a percentage, a version) and every one was outside what the plugin test covered. `names.test.js` now reads every doc and skill and holds each version, command, subcommand, check row, engine call, path and the storyboard header to its source; a wrong name fails the suite.
- Status written in five places is status written in none. By 10 October the kit README, the workspace README, ABOUT and START-HERE described 2 to 6 October while two files knew the truth. Now three files have three jobs (README the two-minute version, ROADMAP the plan, roadmap-and-status this week on one screen) and the orientation files have one copy, reached by links, instead of a mirror kept by hand.
- A promise in a doc is a task or it is a lie. `vkit upgrade` was promised in four places for nine days; 10d was named and never written; the two-minute README was in step 10's own text. The roadmap's "Next, in order" and "Parking lot" are where a promise goes, with what done looks like, and nothing else in the docs says "to come".
- Overwriting the expert's words to satisfy a check loses the thing the fact check judges. The director rewrote the Sentence column by hand (the `sentences-match` row wants the storyboard and the narration equal) and `drift` had nothing on disk to judge against. The command that needs the two equal is the one that writes the column, and it logs what it changed.

- The timeline is built again on every reset, so anything that remembers state across a build (the model's cursor in `act()`) starts fresh at each build or it lies on the second one. The first S3 check on engine 0.6.0 failed `beats-on-screen` with "the screen is buckets-empty, not buckets-one": the second build began where the first ended. A checker row that reads the engine's own record caught it before a frame was looked at.
- A page is not footage. The twin's first cut reused the video's stage: a 1920x1080 frame scaled to fit the window, letterboxed, with the page's scroll simulated inside it. On a laptop it read as a fixed, awkward box that zoom made worse. A page fills the width, scrolls with the browser's own scrollbar, pins its chrome, and takes typing in a real input; the runner does that framing and the states and engine stay as they are. The earlier twin work had the same lesson: the simulated screen is a fluid frame with its own scrolling view, and everything that is not the product sits visibly outside it.
- Coaching lives outside the simulated screen. The earlier twin work drew an explanatory card inside the simulated form once, in a tint and with an icon the product did not have, and a reviewer comparing the build to the capture caught it: a learner cannot tell the explanation from the product, and is then trained on a product that does not exist. Everything that teaches (the step, the why, the fill, the ring, the refusal) belongs in a panel beside the screen, in a colour and a face the product never uses; the screen stays what the captures show. The same work found that a fill button filling every field at once skipped every field's coaching, so it fills one field per click; that an instruction and its why read as one dense block until they were two tiers (do this, bold; because, boxed and optional, hidden when there is nothing to add); and that a learner wandering to a field that is not the step should see the panel's register change ("exploring", amber) rather than nothing. Carried into the sidekick of 12 October 2026 in its own words.
- Pinning a bar from a scroll event shimmers. The compositor scrolls before the main thread runs the scroll handler, so a bar moved by script paints a frame behind, whatever the handler does. Only the compositor can pin: `position: sticky` under CSS `zoom` (a transform scales the stick at the wrong rate, measured 0.4 of the scroll at 0.6), the footer stuck to the top a viewport down (a sticky bottom cannot carry the margin that puts it at the page's end: a sticky element never leaves its containing block, margin included), and no `overflow: hidden` box between the page and the scroller (it is a scroll container, and the bars stick to it instead; `overflow: clip` clips without one). Measure in the first frame after a scroll, not after a wait.
- A copy that nobody lists is a copy that drifts. Before 12 October 2026 two commands each moved one video's copy and nothing said which videos were behind; the S3 video sat on app 0.3.1 while the app was 0.3.2 and only the person who made the bump knew. The hub and its spokes want three things: a listing (`vkit status`), a log of what moved (`CHANGES.md`, written with the bump, never after), and a check that fails on a stale copy (`app-copy-current`), so drift is a failing row and not a memory.
- A click needs no pointer. `click()` returned without pressing when no pointer had been placed, because a video always places one; a learner's hand has none. The press is its own function now and the twin calls it.

## Delivering from a cloud session to the Mac

A session that builds in a sandbox hands its files over a bridge, and each step has bitten once. The order, so it is not re-derived:

1. Fetch and compare first: `git fetch`, `git diff --stat origin/main` against the sandbox tree, so the sandbox builds on what the Mac committed.
2. Stage the files under one batch folder, at most 50 per call, with a checksum list (`shasum -a 256`) written beside them to `_suggested-trash/deliver-<name>.sha256` on the Mac.
3. Verify on the Mac: `shasum -a 256 -c` in the bridge shell. A PNG delivered over the bridge gains a `caBX` chunk and is stripped with a read-modify-write; `packages/cli/bin/vkit.js` loses its execute bit and gets `chmod 755`.
4. A video's old files are copied into `_suggested-trash/<video>.before-<name>.<timestamp>/` before any are overwritten. Never deliver `video.json`; `vkit app use` and `vkit upgrade` write it on the Mac.
5. Matthew runs `PW_CHANNEL=chrome npm test` on the Mac and, when it is green, the commit and push the session wrote out, docs and code in one commit. The GitHub run is the second proof.
6. A plugin change says whether a re-upload is due; `vkit plugin pack` makes the zip.
